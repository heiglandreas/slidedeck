## Altrusim
Note:
Altruism....

Doing things for the benefit of someone else...

If that someone else is a stakeholder, then that's not Altruism!

Like organising or talking at a conference without being paid for it (having costs covered is not being paid.
Just as a short reminder).

Providing information on a blog. Helping others in Forums. Answering emails on Mailinglists. Creating some code and
sharing it with others so thewy can use it to scratch te same itch that made you build the software. Or some other itches.

---
![34789618996_6644ac8c30_k.jpg](deck/resources/34789618996_6644ac8c30_k.jpg)
Note:

Like creating some homepage-tools for the own website and then allowing others to use and modify it. Someone has the
work and someone else can (but might not) benefit from it. Not that I have a specific person in mind. (And just to be
clear: I am talking about the person of the right! - we did have some fun at that conference... )

Or - in general - creating something and then allowing others to use it for free.

Without thinking about monetization first.

This has though some severe drawbacks!
---
![ebe2fa6333dda8b1.jpg](deck/resources/ebe2fa6333dda8b1.jpg)
Note:
For me the main thing about brewing is - exactly  - brewing. Would I want a beer it'd be cheaper and faster to go to
the next store and buy one. Even at 3 Euros a bottle. Just for the maths geeks here: 8 hours at minimum wage plus
20Euros for the grains and 10 euros for the gas I needed for boiling I'D be at around 3,10 euros per bottle.

The beer coming out at the end isn't the main ting. It's just the outcome.  The main thing is the process.
Experimenting. Trying different things. playing around. Learning. Failing! I did brew a few batches that were plain disgusting. (I hope this isn't one of them...)
and I learned A LOT from that.

So as a homebrewer I have the luxury to play around and embrace the process. Tweak a recipe here to see how that ends
up and if it doesn't taste better I just do it differently the next time. No two beers I will brew will taste the same.
Even when I use the exact same ingredients, follow the exact same processes - and if it's only because the raw-materials
are slightly different. And that is for me the fun of it.

---
![18809355860_7515649f58_k.jpg](deck/resources/18809355860_7515649f58_k.jpg)
Note:
All that suddenly stops the moment I start providing that outcome to others. Because people expect a certain stability.
Imagine you buy a bottle of Freiberger or Köstritzer and each time it tastes different. That is not acceptable.

So the moment I move from doing this as a hobby playground for me to provide it as a service to others, everything changes.
Suddenly the outcome becomes the main thing.

And that change happens in at least two regards. One is that it requires time. Time I do no longer have for other things. Like...
earning money for a living. So I either need to cut down on providing this to others. Or I have to find ways to monetizing it.
That is something that the big names have solved neatly. Content providers get actual money for their service - as long as
they are relevant to the service they are on. Go back to being at the mercy of the company.

---
![402.jpg](deck/resources/402.jpg)
Note:
Solving that on ones own becomes much harder and requires more thinking and there is so far no decent solution for that.
Micropayments were one thing that was discussed some time ago but so far no really decent system was created as far as I know.
The challenge might be to build something that is decentralized but still working... Feel free to take that idea!

And the other regard is that it requires commitment.

Suddenly keeping the taste stable is much more important than playing around. Reacting to feedback becomes much more important.
And the actual trying and fiddling around that originally got us started goes more and more to the background.

Just a few weeks ago I read an interesting article by Ryan Cheley Titled "What if Maintainer Burnout isn'T Burnout". And
he cites Dr. Joseph Silletti who wrote an article called "What if burnout isn't the problem" where he describes that doctors
lost "something". And he describes Languising. It's not Burnout (where the tank is dry and you can'T go anymore). It's more
the tank is full. but you have forgotten where you wanted to go.

## What can the community get from me?
Note:
And the answer is: someone that enjoys a beer!

To be honest: Nothing is more frustrating than brewing for an event where you want to provide a beer for everyone and
after the event there is still all the beer left. Minus the five that you drank yourself to numb the frustration that no
one turned up.

Nothing is more frustrating than organising an event and then there is no one turning up.- Or the same 5 people when
you know exactly that there should be at least 500 people in the area interested in the topic.

Being there. Lurking in the shadows. Reading the mailinglist. No one expects you to get up to the podium and do a
4 hour workshop on your first appearance.

That's all.

The community doesn't ask for much. But getting the feedback that it actually matters is a very valuable feedback.

And everyone can provide that easily and actually for free.

## Facebook - and others
Note:
Yes: Facebook can be pretty hard due to the "Everyone is on it" effect and stopping using it would mean loosing contact
with at least some people. The same for Instagram, WhatsApp and LinkedIn. And while there are alternatives, using them
will mean losing out on possibly relevant content and information and loosing an existing network.

Sometimes that can be a win. Most of the time though that's a loss

Should we start building communities based on these platforms with the knowledge we have right now? My take is clear,
but that is something everyone has to answer for themselves. Whichever tool we use, we will lose - or at least
not reach - some people.
---
![Opencollective.jpg](deck/resources/Opencollective.jpg)
Note:
But setting up such alternatives does not always come for free - as in beer. Using phpc.social or phpc.tv -
or - in fact: PHP itself -  is free in general.
But someone has to pay the bills and do the administrative work. There are some people that are chiming in and put together
some bucks to make that happen and also some people are investing their free time for administration and moderation.

So yes: The community can get a lot from you! By acknowledging that what the people are doing is meaningful for you! By
giving feedback! Good and bad (but please always constructive!). By not taking it for granted!
